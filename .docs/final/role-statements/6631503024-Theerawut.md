## What I Owned
As the UI/UX Designer for the **Recovery Score** project, I took full ownership of translating combat-specific recovery requirements into a user-centered mobile prototype tailored for amateur and professional boxers. I initiated the design process by thoroughly reviewing the problem statement and scope documents, mapping out core user journeys, and defining the application's information architecture. My primary design objective was achieving a **low-friction user experience (<90 seconds/day)** so fighters can effortlessly log daily recovery metrics during demanding Fight Camps without abandoning the platform.

To achieve this, I designed streamlined, intuitive input flows covering the 30-second Morning Check-in, Post-Training Log (categorized across four distinct boxing workloads: Sparring, Technical, Conditioning, and Weights), 15-second Nutrition Check, and the 10-second smartphone Punch Test. Furthermore, I structured the main dashboard around the **6-Domain Recovery Engine** (Brain & Impact, Fuel & Weight, Sleep & Heart, Power & Speed, Body, and Mind) integrated with Fight Camp timeline contexts. Rather than displaying overwhelming charts, I crafted clear visual hierarchies that immediately highlight the daily limiting factor, actionable "Train vs. Skip" training directives, and a Guided Recovery Timer. Finally, I ensured that critical safety boundaries—specifically medical diagnosis disclaimers and rapid weight-cutting warnings—were prominently and clearly communicated within the user interface.

## Reflection

### 1. Lessons Learned
Building the Recovery Score app with AI significantly reshaped how I approach software engineering, especially from my role as a UI/UX Designer. AI accelerated early-stage ideation, helping me quickly translate complex combat-specific requirements—like our 6-domain recovery engine and Fight Camp phases—into structured user flows and interactive prototypes. For our team, AI acted as a catalyst for alignment, allowing us to visualize concepts and iterate on feedback much faster during sprints. 

One thing that clearly **got better** was prototyping speed and design exploration; I could rapidly generate and test layouts for our sub-90-second daily check-in workflow without starting from a blank canvas. However, one thing that **got worse** was maintaining domain consistency and design precision. AI-generated outputs often defaulted to generic fitness dashboard templates, missing critical safety boundaries like avoiding medical diagnoses or unsafe weight-cutting calculations. We had to spend extra time manually auditing, refining, and testing every screen to ensure quality, data privacy, and strict alignment with real boxer needs.

### 2. Where I Stand in the Software Industry Today
As a final-year Software Engineering student entering the industry in the AI era, I see myself as a bridge between human needs and AI-assisted execution. AI is a powerful co-pilot, not a substitute for foundational engineering judgment.
