What I Owned

As the AI Lead for the Boxer Recovery project, I took responsibility for overseeing the project’s AI-related direction, establishing appropriate AI implementation strategies, and ensuring that AI-assisted development aligned with the project’s requirements, technical scope, and safety considerations.

My primary responsibility was translating the project’s recovery assessment requirements into a structured and explainable decision-making approach. I worked on defining how the application evaluates an athlete’s recovery condition across six independent domains: Brain & Impact, Fuel & Weight, Sleep & Heart, Power & Speed, Body, and Mind.

A key part of my responsibility was ensuring that the recovery assessment followed a consistent and transparent decision-making process. This included prioritizing the Safety Gate before normal recovery scoring, comparing available metrics against individual recovery baselines, identifying the lowest available domain as the primary recovery limiter, and determining appropriate training recommendations across four categories: Sparring, Technical Boxing, Conditioning, and Strength Training.

I also focused on the responsible use of AI throughout the development process. This involved establishing clear boundaries for AI-assisted work, reviewing AI-generated outputs before they were incorporated into project deliverables, and ensuring that generated information did not introduce unsupported health claims, fabricated test data, or unsafe recommendations.

Furthermore, I contributed to defining the project’s AI strategy by distinguishing between AI-assisted development tools and actual runtime intelligence. The final implementation deliberately uses a deterministic, rule-based recovery engine rather than an AI or machine-learning model at runtime. This decision prioritizes explainability, predictable behavior, testability, and athlete safety within the project’s prototype scope.

I also emphasized technical validation and evidence-based evaluation. The recovery decision rules were supported by dedicated test scenarios, including safety overrides, missing-data handling, personal baseline requirements, and recovery-limiter selection. The project achieved 85/85 passing automated tests, providing evidence that the implemented rules behaved according to their defined specifications.

My overall objective was to ensure that AI was used responsibly and effectively throughout the project while delivering a transparent, testable, and safety-conscious recovery assessment system for boxers.

AI Disclosure

I used AI-assisted development tools, including Claude Code, Gemini, and ChatGPT, to support project analysis, explore implementation approaches, refine technical documentation, and assist with development-related tasks.

AI-generated outputs were treated as supporting materials rather than authoritative results. They were reviewed against the project specifications, recovery scoring rules, and safety requirements before being incorporated into the project. Generated code and rule changes were subject to automated testing and manual review.

Importantly, AI was not used to generate or fabricate user-testing data. The final application uses a deterministic, rule-based engine for runtime recovery assessments rather than an AI-generated diagnosis or machine-learning prediction.
